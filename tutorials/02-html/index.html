COURAGE

“It’s dangerous to go alone. Take this!”
The Legend of Zelda is a series of fantasy-themed roleplay games, the first one being released February 21st, 1986. It went on to sell over 6 million copies (Vega)—cementing it and the rest of its successors as one of the greatest video game series of all time, both in Japan and internationally. Each game in the Zelda series (excluding the most recent Echoes of Wisdom) tells the story of Link, the Hero, fighting against evil in order to save Princess Zelda. The stories in the series are often fantastical, involving the collecting of different artifacts, abilities, and interacting with all sorts of different peoples, civilizations, and races within the world of Zelda.
Prevalent in these games, however, especially through the interaction with other races, is the undercurrent of Orientalism. Orientalism refers to how the Western world views the East, and how their depictions of the East show them as something other, exotic—something to be dominated, or tamed (Beck). While The Legend of Zelda takes place in a fictional setting, many of the peoples that exist within it are clearly modeled after real-world societies and civilizations, or Western depictions of them—often maintaining the Orientalist issues, or viewpoints associated with them.
While this is a critique on The Legend of Zelda series for preserving the problematic views of Orientalism, through pointing out the glaring flaws and stereotypes in its world-building and peoples, this is in no-way an outright attack on Nintendo, either. Instead, this analysis seeks to provide insight into the issues, and inform as to what orientalist views should be avoided in the future, when making new Zelda games, or simply when writing stories in fictional worlds in general. At times, this analysis will also point out instances in which Nintendo did in fact attempt to combat the orientalism prevalent in their stories—both where they succeeded, and where they failed.
Most of The Legend of Zelda stories take place in the fictional world of Hyrule. While some games differ in location, bringing the protagonist, Link, to other countries outside of Hyrule (as in Oracle of Ages and Oracle of Seasons), or different realms all together (such as in Majora’s Mask), the majority of the time, our hero spends his time exploring the world of Hyrule. In Hyrule, the main race of Hylians are inspired by the West—and through the course of the series are shown to be the dominating race of Hyrule. The majority of the architecture present in Hylian cities and towns (shown most in Twilight Princess and Breath of the Wild) also are themed after that of medieval Europe. Aesthetically, Hyrule Castle, in almost every iteration it appears in, is also a clear example of a castle modeled after older European castles.

POWER

Link, a Hylian, is a member of this Western-inspired society. Said to have the Divine Right to rule, due to being descended from the Goddess Hylia herself, many of the Zelda games show the leaders and monarchs of other races as subservient, or of inferior status to that of the Hylian King. This is especially seen in Ocarina of Time, where players are shown how the King of another race, the Gerudo, is forced to bow, or even in a more recent example, Breath of the Wild, where the other races, despite some tensions, are shown to have sworn themselves to the Hylian crown. Domination over others is one part of how Hylians are modeled after the Orientalist view of the Western world—the belief that West has the power, and control over the East, the other, the exotic.
